What happened

A step at the release, not a slow drift.

4.2 shipped on 12 August with three changes that matter here. The week after, the share of pings nobody answered jumped, and some responders stopped being offered work at all.

Ping wait cut

A ping now stays on the phone 60 seconds, down from 90.

Ranking re-weighted

Proximity weight up 0.45 → 0.60. Recent acceptance down 0.40 → 0.25.

What nobody checked

A miss counts as a decline, penalty 0.12 against a 0.08 credit, with no decay. A 2019 TODO says so.

Support only ever explained the second complaint (“gone before I could answer”). The bigger one, “my phone never goes off”, had no explanation until the numbers and code were read together.

The numbers

Missed pings went from 2% to 21% in one week.

Not “August being quiet”. Taken rate sat at 75–78% for six weeks, then fell to 54%. Callout volume dipped a little (about 138 to 110–127 a week), but that is too small to explain it.

Safety stakes

A handler told me of a responder half dressed when a callout moved on. Two of the quiet four's handlers never filed a ticket.
Why it sticks

Quiet is a trap you can't climb out of.

A responder is one number, starting at 0.5. A miss takes off 0.12, a yes adds 0.08, and nothing ever fades. The list stops at the first yes, so a low responder is rarely asked. Try it:

Illustration of a responder who has gone quiet (score 0.00). Dashed line = the 0.5 starting score.
Score 0.00 · yeses in a row: 0 of about 7 needed

But a quiet responder is not being pinged, so there is no callout to take.

Hypothesis The wait cut caused the jump in misses; the 0.12 penalty with no decay turned those misses into lasting quiet. Wen Li hasn't confirmed the stored scores or the penalty, and there are no per-ping answer times yet. The 4.2 weight change alone should have weakened this loop, not strengthened it. This is the leading explanation, not a proven one.

Making sure it doesn't happen again

A six-point check, run the same way every time.

Priya told me she “made calls faster than I checked them.” Bulk Callout shipped in 4.1 without the baseline its brief asked for. The review-checklist skill checks a brief before it goes any further, and a scheduled task runs it every Monday at about 9:00 on my brief.

1
OwnerOne named person, not a team or “TBD”.
2
How we'll know it workedSuccess in plain terms, not just what gets built.
3
Scope matchesThe end commits to what the start promised.
4
Problem before fixEvidence first, solution second.
5
Success metricNamed, with a baseline and a target.
6
TimelineDates or a release, with a milestone.

My brief, first run: 1 of 6

Owner fail · how we'll know partial · scope partial · problem-first pass · metric fail · timeline partial

Fix first: name an owner, pick one metric with a baseline and target, define “quiet”.

Why this would have caught 4.2

The weekly average looked fine because nobody had named a per-responder metric with a baseline. Check 5 forces that, and check 2 forces “how will we know?” before shipping. It checks the brief, not the code, so it won't catch a bad penalty value by itself.
